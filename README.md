# WirWarten

> Turning invisible healthcare demand into something that can actually be acted on.

## The problem

Imagine you need a specialist.

You call 10 practices. Then 20. Then 30.

No appointments. No new patients. Maybe the next available slot is months away.

The system's answer is usually:

**Keep searching.**

Which makes the problem look individual: *you simply haven't found the right doctor yet.*

But sometimes that isn't the real problem.

Maybe **50 people in the same area are all searching for exactly the same specialist**.

For some specialties, getting an appointment within a reasonable time can feel closer to winning the lottery than accessing a functioning service.

## The idea

**Wir warten** makes that hidden demand visible.

Instead of everyone searching alone, people can see healthcare access problems already reported around them.

For example:

> **Dermatologist accepting publicly insured patients — Heilbronn**  
> 52 people waiting

If this is also your problem, you simply click:

**I'm waiting too.**

If your problem isn't there, you add it.

The result is not another doctor directory.

It is a map of **unmet healthcare demand**.

## Why aggregation matters

50 people making 50 separate phone calls are mostly noise to the system.

50 people represented as one clear demand signal are something different:

> There are 50 people in this area currently waiting for the same specialist.

At that point, it may become reasonable to approach a clinic, insurer, association or other healthcare institution with a concrete proposal:

> Could you open one additional consultation day and handle this group together?

For a clinic, organizing one exceptional batch of appointments may even be easier than receiving and processing dozens of unrelated calls over several months.

The goal is to transform:

**50 isolated problems**

into:

**1 measurable demand that somebody can respond to.**

## What the first version looks like

Think of something closer to Slido than a traditional healthcare portal.

You open the page and immediately see the healthcare access problems around you.

You can:

- see what other people are waiting for
- click **I'm waiting too**
- add a missing access problem
- see how many people share the same problem

That's it.

No medical records.  
No doctor reviews.  
No appointment marketplace.

The first question we want to answer is much simpler:

**Can aggregating healthcare demand make it easier for institutions to respond to it?**

## What I want feedback on

This is still an experiment.

I'm especially interested in challenging:

- whether the demand aggregation mechanism makes sense
- what would make the numbers credible enough for institutions
- who should receive the aggregated demand
- what incentives clinics or insurers would have to respond
- where this model breaks
- which healthcare access problems would be best for an initial test

If the idea interests you, open an issue, start a discussion, or reach out.

Feedback that kills a bad assumption early is as useful as help building the project.

## Status

Early prototype / concept validation.

The immediate goal is not to build a large platform.

It is to find one real healthcare access problem, aggregate enough demand around it, and test whether that changes what becomes possible.
